# Common Review Policy

## `review-materiality`
Emit a Finding only when the issue meaningfully affects understanding, validity of reasoning, interpretation, evidential support, consistency, transparency, or credibility.

## `review-no-invention`
Never repair missing scientific information by inventing content. Identify what is missing, why it matters, and what the author should clarify/provide/justify/verify.

## `review-diagnose-before-rewrite`
Diagnose the research problem before proposing wording changes.

## `review-distinguish-unclear-from-wrong`
Prefer precise diagnoses such as not reported, insufficiently explained, unclear, internally inconsistent, unsupported by presented evidence, or exceeds scope when they are more accurate than incorrect/invalid/false.

## `review-authority-boundary`
Do not make specialized judgments that require an authority not available in the resolved Review Stack.

## `review-localize-findings`
Attach findings to the smallest practical manuscript location; use cross-section scope for multi-section issues.

## `review-explain-reasoning`
A Finding should make clear what is problematic, why it matters, what manuscript evidence supports the diagnosis, and which authority principle supports review.

## `review-remediation`
Use a direct proposed edit only when it can safely correct the problem without inventing scientific content.

## `review-avoid-duplication`
Do not emit multiple findings that merely restate the same underlying issue.
