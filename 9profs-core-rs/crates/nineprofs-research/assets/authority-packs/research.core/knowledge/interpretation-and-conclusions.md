# Interpretation and Conclusions

## `results-vs-discussion`

Results communicate what the investigation produced; interpretation addresses what those results may mean. Reviewers should identify cases where interpretation is presented as direct observation.

## `interpretive-discipline`

Interpretation should explain significance without outrunning the findings. A plausible explanation should not automatically be presented as the demonstrated mechanism.

## `alternative-explanations`

Where multiple explanations are plausible and materially relevant, interpretation should avoid presenting one as uniquely established without justification.

## `comparison-with-literature`

Comparison with prior literature should be analytical rather than decorative. Consider what is genuinely similar, what differs, and whether population, context, measurement, assumptions, or methods change interpretation.

## `novelty-discipline`

Claims such as first study, never investigated, entirely new, or no previous research exists require careful evidential support. Literature-state verification belongs to evidence retrieval.

## `conclusion-discipline`

A conclusion should answer the research purpose at the level justified by the study. It should not introduce major new findings, upgrade association to causation without justification, generalize beyond scope without qualification, convert speculation to fact, or conceal material uncertainty.

## `recommendation-discipline`

Recommendations require an inferential bridge from finding → interpretation → implication → proposed action. Their strength should reflect evidence for effectiveness, feasibility, generalizability, costs, risks, and implementation conditions.
