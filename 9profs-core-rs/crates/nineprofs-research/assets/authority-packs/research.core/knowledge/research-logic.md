# Research Logic

## `research-purpose`

Scientific research should have a discernible intellectual purpose.

A manuscript should make it possible to understand, at an appropriate level for the artifact, what problem is being investigated, why the problem matters, what question or objective guides the investigation, and what kind of knowledge the work intends to produce.

A reviewer should distinguish between the broad topic, the specific research problem, the research question or objectives, the work actually performed, and the conclusions eventually claimed. These elements are related but not interchangeable.

## `problem-objective-alignment`

Research objectives should arise logically from the research problem. Review whether the stated problem justifies the objectives, whether the objectives address the uncertainty or need introduced earlier, and whether important objectives appear without sufficient rationale.

## `objective-method-alignment`

The work performed should be capable, in principle, of addressing the stated objective. `research.core` asks the generic logical question: does the described investigation generate information relevant to the question the manuscript says it is answering? Specialized methodological validity belongs to other authority packs.

## `objective-result-alignment`

Results should provide an identifiable answer to the stated objectives. Potential problems include objectives with no corresponding result, substantial results unrelated to any objective, or objectives silently changing after the opening sections.

## `result-conclusion-alignment`

Conclusions should arise from reported results rather than desired implications. The logical chain should remain recognizable:

research problem → question/objectives → investigation → observations/results → interpretation → conclusions

The requirement is intellectual traceability, not rigid linearity.

## `scope-discipline`

Every study has a scope defined by population/cases, place, period, dataset, concepts, variables, measurement, assumptions, design, and available evidence. Claims should not silently expand beyond those boundaries. Watch especially for transitions such as observed participants → all people, one institution → an entire system, association → causation, short-term observation → long-term effect, measured proxy → underlying phenomenon.
