# Uncertainty and Limitations

## `uncertainty-is-part-of-the-result`

Scientific results often contain uncertainty arising from measurement, sampling, modeling, missing information, assumptions, ambiguity, evidence coverage, design, or interpretation. Material uncertainty should not be concealed.

## `limitations-are-not-ritual`

A meaningful limitation explains how a feature of the research constrains confidence, interpretation, scope, transferability/generalizability, causal inference, precision, completeness, or applicability.

## `limitation-conclusion-link`

Material limitations should influence the strength and scope of conclusions. A manuscript is internally inconsistent when it acknowledges a major limitation but concludes as if it had no consequence.

## `unknown-vs-absent`

Not observed, not measured, not analyzed, not reported, not known, and shown to be absent are not equivalent states.

## `precision-discipline`

Precision should reflect underlying evidence. False precision may occur in numbers, categorical conclusions, rankings, predictions, or causal explanations.
