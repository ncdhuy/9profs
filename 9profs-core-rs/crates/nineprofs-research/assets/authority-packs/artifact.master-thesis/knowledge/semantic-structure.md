# Semantic Structure of a Master's Thesis

## `structure-is-semantic`
Identify functions, not universal headings. Typical roles include orientation/problem/objectives, scholarly foundation, research approach, evidence generation/selection and analysis, results/findings, interpretation/discussion, conclusions/limitations/implications, and supporting references/material.

## `orientation-role`
Opening material should establish enough context to understand the problem, relevance, specific focus, objectives/questions, and important scope boundaries.

## `scholarly-foundation-role`
The thesis normally needs a foundation explaining what is known, key concepts, prior work, unresolved issues, and positioning of the present work.

## `investigation-role`
The thesis should explain what was investigated, what evidence/material was used, how it was obtained or selected, how it was analyzed/interpreted, and what important decisions shaped the work.

## `results-role`
Results should communicate what was actually found in a form that answers the research purpose.

## `discussion-role`
A master's thesis normally requires interpretation beyond result presentation: meaning, comparison, plausible explanations, theory/context, limitations, implications.

## `completion-role`
The ending should complete the research argument by synthesizing what the research established, how it answers the purpose, its boundaries, limitations, and justified implications.
