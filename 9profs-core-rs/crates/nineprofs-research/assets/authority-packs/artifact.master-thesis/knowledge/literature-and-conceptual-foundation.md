# Literature and Conceptual Foundation

## `literature-has-a-function`
Literature should serve the research: define the problem, establish what is known, identify unresolved questions, explain concepts, justify choices, compare findings, or build conceptual/theoretical framing.

## `synthesis-over-catalogue`
A thesis should usually synthesize patterns, agreements, disagreements, methodological/contextual differences, gaps, and conceptual relationships rather than catalogue source summaries.

## `relevance-to-research`
Literature coverage should be relevant to the actual thesis; quantity alone does not establish adequacy.

## `gap-discipline`
A research gap should connect logically to the research purpose and should not be treated as a ritual statement.

## `conceptual-clarity`
Important concepts should be defined or situated sufficiently for the reader to understand their role. Frameworks should meaningfully organize the question, variables, analysis, or interpretation.

## `literature-currentness-is-contextual`
No universal age limit for references is imposed; currentness depends on field and purpose.
