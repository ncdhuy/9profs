# Contribution and Completion

## `contribution-is-contextual`
A master's thesis should make a discernible contribution appropriate to scale; this may be empirical, contextual, replicative, synthetic, methodological, analytical, practical, or conceptual.

## `contribution-should-be-earned`
Contribution claims should follow from the work performed and remain at a justified scale.

## `completion-not-repetition`
The conclusion should synthesize problem, objectives, major findings, interpretation, limitations, and contribution rather than mechanically repeat.

## `recommendations-are-conditional`
Recommendations should emerge logically from findings and implications and need not appear in every thesis.

## `limitations-demonstrate-maturity`
Thoughtful limitations can demonstrate scholarly maturity when their consequences are explained.

## `future-research`
Future research suggestions should arise from genuine unresolved issues rather than generic boilerplate.
