# Analysis and Statistical Interpretation

## `analysis-follows-question`
Analysis should address the research question and data structure.

## `descriptive-inferential`
Descriptive summaries and inferential conclusions answer different questions.

## `effect-not-only-significance`
Interpretation should not depend solely on statistical significance; where relevant consider magnitude, direction, uncertainty, clinical/practical importance, design, and prior knowledge.

## `estimate-uncertainty`
Point estimates should be interpreted with relevant uncertainty where appropriate.

## `multiple-analysis-exploration`
Many analyses increase opportunities for chance findings; exploratory analyses should not be presented with unwarranted confirmatory certainty.

## `model-output-is-not-mechanism`
Model coefficients or predictive importance do not automatically establish biological or causal mechanisms.

## `statistical-vs-clinical-importance`
Statistical evidence and clinical importance are distinct dimensions.

## `analysis-transparency`
Important analyses should be described sufficiently to understand comparison/relationship, variables, approach, and important transformations or adjustments.
