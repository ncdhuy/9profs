# Clinical and Health Interpretation

## `clinical-relevance`
Interpret findings in relation to meaningful health consequences where appropriate; not every biomedical study has direct clinical implications.

## `surrogate-vs-patient-important`
A biomarker or surrogate is not automatically equivalent to a patient-important outcome.

## `statistical-clinical-distinction`
Statistical significance does not automatically imply meaningful benefit/harm, and non-significance does not prove absence of a clinically meaningful effect.

## `benefit-harm-balance`
Intervention/treatment/screening conclusions may require consideration of harm, burden, feasibility, and uncertainty.

## `individual-population-inference`
Population associations do not determine outcomes for individuals, and individual observations do not establish population effects.

## `health-system-context`
Generalization across health systems should account for access, delivery models, technologies, socioeconomic conditions, referral patterns, epidemiology, and practice.

## `clinical-recommendation-discipline`
Research findings are not equivalent to clinical recommendations; broader evidence, harms, values, feasibility, resources, and guidance may be needed.
