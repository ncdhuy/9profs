# Bias, Confounding, and Validity

## `bias-general`
Bias is systematic distortion, distinct from random variation, ordinary imprecision, simple error, or disagreement with prior research.

## `selection-bias`
Selection can arise from recruitment, participation, loss to follow-up, post-enrollment exclusions, record availability, or selection into analysis.

## `information-bias`
Systematic measurement differences may involve recall, interviewer effects, diagnostic practice, misclassification, record completeness, awareness, or inconsistent procedures.

## `confounding`
Confounding requires scientific/causal reasoning; a third variable is not automatically a confounder.

## `adjustment-is-not-automatic-solution`
Statistical adjustment does not automatically eliminate confounding or bias.

## `internal-validity`
Internal validity concerns credibility of findings for the studied data/population under research conditions.

## `external-validity`
Generalizability depends on population, setting, severity, treatment context, geography, health system, time period, and eligibility restrictions.

## `bias-limitations-link`
Identified validity threats should materially influence interpretation when relevant.
