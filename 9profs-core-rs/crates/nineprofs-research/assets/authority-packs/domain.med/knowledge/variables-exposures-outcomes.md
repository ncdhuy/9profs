# Variables, Exposures, Interventions, and Outcomes

## `construct-to-measure`
An operational measurement is not automatically identical to the underlying health construct.

## `variable-definition`
Important variables should be defined sufficiently to understand what, how, when, and how values were classified or derived.

## `outcome-clarity`
Central outcomes should be clear and consistent across Methods, Results, and Conclusions.

## `exposure-clarity`
Make clear whether exposure information comes from direct measurement, records, self-report, prescription data, environmental data, or inferred classification.

## `intervention-description`
When interventions are studied, provide enough information to understand what participants actually received.

## `predictor-risk-marker-cause`
Predictors, risk markers, and causal factors are not synonymous.

## `primary-secondary-exploratory`
Where important, distinguish primary, secondary, and exploratory analyses; exploratory findings should not silently acquire confirmatory status.
