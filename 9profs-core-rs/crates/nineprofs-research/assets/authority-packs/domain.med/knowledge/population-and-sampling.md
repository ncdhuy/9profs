# Population and Sampling

## `target-study-sample`
Distinguish target population → source population → eligible population → recruited/included participants → analyzed participants when consequential.

## `population-definition`
Population should be defined sufficiently for interpreting to whom or what results apply.

## `inclusion-exclusion`
Eligibility criteria influence selection, safety, comparability, measurement quality, representativeness, and generalizability; long lists are not automatically rigorous.

## `sampling-logic`
Interpret sampling relative to intended inference. Ask how participants/records were identified, from what population, and whether inclusion could systematically favor characteristics.

## `sample-size-purpose`
Sample-size reasoning depends on purpose (estimate, compare, detect effect, model, diagnosis, qualitative exploration, complete population). No universal formula is imposed.

## `attrition-and-analysis-population`
Withdrawal, loss to follow-up, missing data, post-recruitment exclusions, protocol deviations, or unavailable measurements can change the analyzed population and interpretation.
